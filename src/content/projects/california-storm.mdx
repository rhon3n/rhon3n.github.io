## Overview

California Storm's site held years of useful program information, but it was spread across page builders, shortcodes, plugins, and legacy navigation. The rebuild kept WordPress as the editorial source of truth and gave the club a clearer public experience around the information families use most: schedules, teams, tryouts, applications, and program history.

The work covered product design, front-end development, content migration, WordPress plugin architecture, and operational handoff.

## The challenge

A visual update alone would not solve the underlying problem. The site needed to become easier to navigate without losing the data and publishing habits the club already relied on.

That meant preserving established sources such as TablePress, Google Calendar, forms, commerce records, and WordPress content while reducing duplicate destinations and fragile manual updates. It also meant making the experience work for parents and players who often need information from a phone at practice, at a tournament, or on the way to tryouts.

## The approach

The project began with an inventory of 104 published pages. Each destination was classified as canonical, redirect, archive, retain, or review. Existing data sources stayed authoritative, while the interfaces around them were rebuilt for search, filtering, readability, and responsive use.

The front end paired the club's established blue identity with a new system built around Barlow typography, sharp court geometry, strong photography, and focused accent color. Mobile layouts were designed as their own experience, with readable schedules, compact navigation, touch-sized controls, and reduced-motion support.

Underneath the interface, the implementation separated editorial configuration, automation, page rendering, and the flagship landing experience into four purpose-built WordPress plugins. Those boundaries let the club update content without reopening the design and make changes without tying every concern to a single theme.

## What changed

The redesigned experience gives families clearer paths to teams, dates, applications, and contact information. A regional tryouts hub replaces scattered application pages with seven regional paths and contextual dates. Tournament schedules retain their existing TablePress source while becoming searchable, filterable, and easier to scan on smaller screens. Legacy and alumni content keeps its original records while gaining a more consistent editorial presentation.

The publishing experience is more deliberate, too. Structured fields, media controls, focal-position settings, previews, revision history, dry runs, and rollback artifacts reduce the need for one-off code changes when content arrives.

## Handoff

The final handoff includes a WordPress guide for day-to-day editing, data ownership, publishing, rollbacks, and launch procedures. It also defines the systems future developers or agents need to inspect before making a change: the current source of truth, the relevant template, the expected verification path, and the rollback option.

The handoff leaves the organization with a system it can operate and evolve.
