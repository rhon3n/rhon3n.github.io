## Overview

California Storm's site held years of program information across page builders, shortcodes, plugins, and legacy navigation. The rebuild kept WordPress as the editorial source and made the information families use most easier to find: schedules, teams, tryouts, applications, and program history.

The work covered product design, front-end development, content migration, WordPress plugin architecture, and handoff.

## The challenge

A visual update was not enough. The site needed clearer navigation without losing the data and publishing habits the club relied on.

The rebuild kept sources such as TablePress, Google Calendar, forms, commerce records, and WordPress content while reducing duplicate destinations and fragile manual updates. It also had to work for parents and players checking information from a phone at practice, tournaments, or tryouts.

## The approach

The project began with an inventory of 104 published pages. Each page was marked as canonical, redirect, archive, retain, or review. Existing data sources stayed authoritative while the interface was rebuilt for search, filtering, readability, and smaller screens.

The front end kept the club's blue identity and added Barlow typography, court geometry, photography, and focused accent color. Mobile layouts were designed separately, with readable schedules, compact navigation, touch-sized controls, and reduced-motion support.

The implementation separated editorial configuration, automation, page rendering, and the landing experience into four WordPress plugins. This let the club update content without reopening the design or tying every concern to one theme.

## What changed

Families have clearer paths to teams, dates, applications, and contact information. A regional tryouts hub replaces scattered application pages with seven regional paths and relevant dates. Tournament schedules keep their TablePress source but are searchable, filterable, and easier to scan on smaller screens. Legacy and alumni content keeps its original records with a more consistent presentation.

Structured fields, media controls, focal-position settings, previews, revision history, dry runs, and rollback artifacts reduce the number of one-off code changes needed when content arrives.

## Handoff

The handoff includes a WordPress guide for day-to-day editing, data ownership, publishing, rollbacks, and launch procedures. It identifies what a future developer or agent needs to inspect before making a change: the current source, the relevant template, the verification path, and the rollback option.

The club can operate and extend the site without rebuilding it from scratch.
